Varun Manjunath

PhD candidate, Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Southern California

I work on efficient inference of LLMs, multimodal models and sparse DNNs, from wafer-scale serving to energy-constrained edge accelerators. I am advised by Prof. Priyadarshini Panda in the Intelligent Computing Lab at USC.

The problem I keep returning to: work that looks uniform to the hardware is not. Bit-serial operand pairs, sparse output elements, and the encode, prefill and decode stages of a multimodal request carry unequal cost, and the straggler sets the makespan or misses the deadline. I look for the finest granularity at which work can be independently assigned, and reach it through hardware–software co-design.

I began my PhD at Yale University in August 2025 and moved to USC in January 2026 along with my advisor, Prof. Panda. Before that, I completed a B.Tech (Honours) and M.Tech in Electrical Engineering at IIT Madras, advised by Prof. Gopalakrishnan Srinivasan.

BRIM: Workload-Balanced Dual-Sided Bit-Serial Sparse Inference Accelerator

Varun Manjunath, Ruokai Yin, Donghyun Lee, Arkapravo Ghosh, Priyadarshini Panda

ICCAD 2026

Dual-sided bit-serial sparse accelerators cap PE utilization at 56–64% because concurrent weight–activation pairs carry unequal work. Balanced pruning plus lightweight runtime slot donation lifts utilization above 90%, for up to 2.37× speedup across CNNs, ViTs and LLMs.

NeuroFlex: Lossless Element-Level ANN–SNN Co-Execution for Efficient Sparse Inference

Varun Manjunath, Pranav Ramesh, Gopalakrishnan Srinivasan

MICRO 2026

Hybrid ANN–SNN accelerators route work per layer or tile even though per-element cost varies several-fold. Proving integer-exact ANN–SNN equivalence per output element enables element-level routing, with 57–67% lower EDP and bit-identical outputs.
